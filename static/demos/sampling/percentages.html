What this simulation assumes

A fixed, fictional population

All 50,000 students have a fixed yes/no opinion. Changing the population settings changes that population and clears the samples. Real researchers usually do not know the population answer.

Every selected student responds truthfully. There is no nonresponse or measurement error here.

Selection and estimation

Each draw samples without replacement. A student can appear again in a later, independent sample.

The convenience scenario fixes the sample mix at approximately 70% first-year, 20% middle-year, and 10% seniors, then samples randomly within each group. It illustrates one specific source of selection bias, not every kind of convenience sample.

Reading the charts

Dots are stacked in narrow bins; their horizontal positions are rounded for display. The reported estimates are not rounded for calculations. All setups share the same horizontal scale.

Weighted stratified estimates combine group results using population shares. An unweighted equal-group estimate can target the wrong overall mix. An uncertainty interval does not capture selection bias.
